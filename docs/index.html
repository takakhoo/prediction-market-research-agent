The Shrinking Edge

A Polymarket contract that pays $1 if Bitcoin is above a strike at noon is a digital option. Below, a textbook formula prices one real contract at every one of its 6,998 fills, from public spot data alone. Scrub through the last 36 hours, bend the formula's volatility, and watch where it agrees with the market, where it disagrees, and what following it would have cost.

Inputs the formula sees at the cursor: the last one-minute candle that had closed, the time to the settlement candle named in the contract's rules, and realized volatility over a trailing window about four times the horizon. Nothing is fitted. The volatility slider multiplies that trailing estimate so you can see how much the verdict depends on it. The fill at the cursor is the real one; the tally counts every earlier fill the rule would have taken at the current settings and what those fills made when the contract settled.

2. The formula

value of Yes = Φ(d2),   d2 = ( ln(S / K) − σ2τ / 2 ) / ( σ √τ )

S is spot, K the strike, τ the time to settlement in years, σ annualized volatility, and Φ the standard normal distribution function. The rule takes a fill when the side the taker bought is worth at least the bar more than its price plus the fee, where the fee is rate × price × (1 − price) per share.
